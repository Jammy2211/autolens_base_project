# Potential corrections (gravitational imaging)

Every lens mass model in the workspace is smooth by construction. A `PowerLaw` or an
`Isothermal` varies gradually across the image plane, because that is what a handful of
parameters can express. Real lens galaxies are not smooth: they carry dark matter subhaloes,
satellites and globular clusters, and their large-scale mass distributions depart from
ellipsoidal symmetry in ways no parametric profile captures.

Gravitational imaging reconstructs those departures directly instead of guessing their form.
Small pixelized corrections $\delta\psi$ to the *lensing potential* live on a coarse regular
mesh over the image plane, and are solved for linearly alongside the pixelized source.
Wherever the smooth model fails to fit the arcs, the corrections absorb the discrepancy — and
their curvature

$\delta\kappa = \frac{1}{2}\nabla^2\delta\psi$

is a map of the missing (or excess) convergence. This is the method that found a
$\sim 10^6 M_\odot$ object at cosmological distance in the VLBI imaging of JVAS B1938+666
(Powell et al. 2025, Nat. Astron. 9, 1714; Vegetti et al. 2026) — the smallest dark object
ever found by lensing.

The statistical framing matters as much as the physics. Both the source and $\delta\psi$ are
regularized, and the strengths of *both* regularizations are set by maximising the Bayesian
evidence rather than by hand. That is what stops the corrections from simply fitting the
noise: a solution flexible enough to absorb noise pays for it in the evidence's complexity
terms. Read
[`wiki/core/concepts/substructure_and_subhalos.md`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PyAutoLabs/autolens_assistant/main/wiki/core/concepts/substructure_and_subhalos.md)
for how this sits against flux-ratio anomalies and grid-search detection, and
[`wiki/core/concepts/inversions_and_pixelizations.md`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PyAutoLabs/autolens_assistant/main/wiki/core/concepts/inversions_and_pixelizations.md)
for the linear-inversion and evidence machinery the corrections reuse.

The implementation (`al.pc`, `PyAutoLens:autolens/potential_correction/`) is ported from the
`potential_correction` package of Cao et al. 2025
(https://github.com/caoxiaoyue/lensing_potential_correction). **If you use it, cite Cao et
al. 2025** — citation materials are at https://github.com/caoxiaoyue/potential_correction_paper.

## Ask

Before writing anything, find out where the user actually is:

- *"Do you have a converged smooth-model fit, and what is its chi-squared per degree of
  freedom?"* This is the gate, not a formality — see the first branch below.
- *"Is your source parametric or already pixelized?"* It decides the `SrcFactory`:
  `al.pc.AnalyticSrcFactory` wraps a parametric source galaxy, `al.pc.PixSrcFactoryITP`
  interpolates a previous pixelized reconstruction.
- *"Are you hunting a compact perturber, or checking for extended departures from your
  smooth model?"* Both are in scope — but it changes the regularization you want, and it
  changes whether the iterative engine is worth its cost.
- *"Imaging or interferometer data?"* Visibility-space work takes the sparse-operator route
  and has its own entry point.
- *"Do you already know your regularization strengths, or should the evidence choose them?"*
  In a real analysis, the evidence should choose.

## Branch — the regime gate (do this first)

Corrections measure *residual* mass structure. If your source model is wrong, the
corrections will happily absorb source-model error and hand you a $\delta\kappa$ map full of
structure that is not mass. So check the smooth-model fit first, and be willing to stop here.

The published pipelines and the PyAutoLens validation campaign both use the same rule of
thumb: the smooth-model source fit should reach $\chi^2$/dof of roughly **1-3**. Outside that
band, go back and improve the source model — [`al_adaptive_pixelization`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PyAutoLabs/autolens_assistant/main/skills/al_adaptive_pixelization.md)
and [`al_inspect_source_reconstruction`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PyAutoLabs/autolens_assistant/main/skills/al_inspect_source_reconstruction.md) are the tools
for that — before spending compute on corrections.

```python
"""
__Regime Gate__

Potential corrections only measure residual mass structure once the source model already
fits the data to near the noise. We check chi-squared per degree of freedom on the smooth
model fit before going further; outside ~1-3 the corrections would absorb source-model
error rather than real mass structure.
"""
fit_smooth = al.FitImaging(dataset=masked_imaging, tracer=tracer_smooth)

print(f"smooth-model reduced chi^2 = {fit_smooth.reduced_chi_squared:.2f} (want ~1-3)")
```

Ask the user what they get before moving on. If it is 15, the answer is not "turn up the
regularization" — it is a better source model.

## Branch — masking to the arcs

The corrections respond to the data through the *source brightness gradients*, which vanish
away from the arcs. Constrain $\delta\psi$ where there is signal and nowhere else, or the
unconstrained region will be filled in by the regularization alone.

`al.pc.util.arc_mask_from` (`PyAutoLens:autolens/potential_correction/util.py`) thresholds
the signal-to-noise map, drops small disconnected islands, dilates the result and cleans it
so every unmasked pixel can support the finite-difference derivative operators.

```python
"""
__Arc Mask__

Gravitational imaging only constrains the potential where the lensed arcs are. We threshold
the signal-to-noise map into an arc-tracing region; `ext_size` dilates it so the finite
difference derivative operators have valid neighbours at the boundary.
"""
mask_array = al.pc.util.arc_mask_from(
    np.asarray(dataset.signal_to_noise_map.native),
    threshold=3.0,
    ignore_size=25,
    ext_size=5,
)
mask = al.Mask2D(mask=mask_array, pixel_scales=dataset.pixel_scales)
masked_imaging = dataset.apply_mask(mask=mask)
```

## Branch — the one-shot joint inversion

`al.pc.FitDpsiSrcImaging` inverts the image jointly for the pixelized source and the
corrections, fully accounting for their covariance. This is the cheap entry point and where
almost everyone should start.

Two objects configure it. `al.pc.DpsiPixelization` holds the corrections' mesh
(`al.pc.RegularDpsiMesh(factor=2)` — a mesh twice as coarse as the data grid) and their
regularization. The source pixelization is any standard PyAutoLens pixelization.

The regularization choice is a real scientific decision, not a default to accept.
`al.reg.MaternKernel` (with `nu=2.5`) permits both localised and extended perturbations,
which is the key advance of Cao et al. 2025 over curvature-only schemes;
`al.reg.CurvatureMask` and `al.reg.FourthOrderMask` are the classic alternatives and bias
you toward smooth, extended corrections.

```python
"""
__Joint Inversion__

`FitDpsiSrcImaging` solves for the pixelized source and the potential corrections in one
linear system, so their covariance is handled exactly. The MaternKernel regularization on
dpsi permits both compact and extended perturbations; the classic CurvatureMask and
FourthOrderMask alternatives favour smooth ones. See
`PyAutoLens:autolens/potential_correction/fit.py`.
"""
dpsi_pixelization = al.pc.DpsiPixelization(
    mesh=al.pc.RegularDpsiMesh(factor=2),
    regularization=al.reg.MaternKernel(coefficient=2000.0, scale=4.0, nu=2.5),
)

fit = al.pc.FitDpsiSrcImaging(
    masked_imaging=masked_imaging,
    lens_start=lens_smooth,
    source_start=al.pc.AnalyticSrcFactory(source_galaxy=source_smooth),
    dpsi_pixelization=dpsi_pixelization,
    src_pixelization=src_pixelization,
    src_image_mesh=src_image_mesh,
)

print(f"joint source + dpsi log evidence = {fit.log_evidence:.4e}")
```

## Branch — reading the dkappa map

`fit.best_fit_dpsi` is the potential correction. Its physical meaning is clearest after
applying the mesh's Laplacian, which turns it into a convergence correction. A dark subhalo
the smooth model omits shows up as a positive $\delta\kappa$ peak at its position.

```python
"""
__Dkappa Map__

The corrections live on the coarse dpsi mesh. Applying the mesh Laplacian converts them to
a convergence correction, dkappa = 0.5 * grad^2 dpsi — the quantity with a direct mass
interpretation. A perturber the smooth model omits appears as a positive peak.
"""
dkappa = fit.pair_dpsi_data_obj.hamiltonian_dpsi @ fit.best_fit_dpsi

peak = np.argmax(dkappa)
print(
    f"dkappa peak at (y, x) = ({fit.pair_dpsi_data_obj.ygrid_dpsi_1d[peak]:.2f}, "
    f"{fit.pair_dpsi_data_obj.xgrid_dpsi_1d[peak]:.2f})"
)

al.pc.visualize.show_fit_dpsi_src(fit, output=str(PLOT_DIR / "joint_fit.png"))
print(f"Saved to: {PLOT_DIR.resolve()}")
```

Note the `al.pc` visualization entry points (`al.pc.visualize.show_fit_dpsi_src`,
`show_fit_dpsi`) take a single `output=` path rather than the
`output_path`/`output_filename`/`output_format` triple used by the `autolens.plot` API in
[`wiki/core/api/plotting.md`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PyAutoLabs/autolens_assistant/main/wiki/core/api/plotting.md) — they are a subpackage-local
convention. Quote the saved path back to the user and offer to open it (`xdg-open` on Linux,
`explorer.exe` from WSL).

Interpretation is where users go wrong. A peak is not automatically a detection: check that
it is not sitting on the arc's brightest gradient (where the method is most sensitive to
source-model error), and confirm it survives a change of regularization.

## Branch — iterative refinement

The one-shot fit linearizes the corrections around the smooth model once.
`al.pc.IterFitDpsiSrcImaging` follows Powell et al. 2025: it optimizes the combined
[source | dpsi] state with a Levenberg-Marquardt loop and, after every accepted step,
**re-ray-traces the image grid through the corrected lens**. The corrections feed back into
the source mapping, which captures compact perturbers more faithfully than one linearization.

`gauge_constraints=True` removes the degeneracies inherent to potential corrections — a
constant $\delta\psi$ and linear gradients just shift the source and are unconstrained by the
data — by enforcing $\langle\delta\psi, 1\rangle = \langle\delta\psi, x\rangle = \langle\delta\psi, y\rangle = 0$
at every step. Leave it on.

```python
"""
__Iterative Refinement__

The Levenberg-Marquardt engine re-ray-traces through the corrected lens after each accepted
step, so the corrections feed back into the source mapping. gauge_constraints removes the
constant and linear-gradient modes that the data cannot constrain. See
`PyAutoLens:autolens/potential_correction/iterative.py`.
"""
iter_fit = al.pc.IterFitDpsiSrcImaging(
    masked_imaging=masked_imaging,
    lens_start=lens_smooth,
    dpsi_pixelization=dpsi_pixelization,
    src_pixelization=src_pixelization,
    src_image_mesh=src_image_mesh,
    gauge_constraints=True,
    n_iter=5,
)

s_opt, dpsi_opt = iter_fit.solve_joint_optimization()
print(f"iterative Laplace log evidence = {iter_fit.log_evidence():.4e}")
```

The dense linear algebra runs through `al.pc.dense_util`, whose kernels follow the PyAuto
`xp` convention — pass `xp=jax.numpy` to `solve_joint_optimization` to run them under JAX on
an accelerator. The default is numpy.

## Branch — letting the evidence choose the regularization

The coefficients and scales above are not known in a real analysis. Sample them with a
non-linear search, using the inversion's Bayesian evidence as the likelihood, via
`al.pc.DpsiSrcInvAnalysis` (one-shot) or `al.pc.IterDpsiSrcInvAnalysis` (iterative).

```python
"""
__Evidence Sampling__

The regularization hyper-parameters are sampled rather than fixed, with the inversion's
Bayesian evidence as the likelihood. This is what makes the result objective: an over-flexible
correction is penalised by the evidence's complexity terms.
"""
dpsi_model = af.Model(
    al.pc.DpsiSrcPixelization,
    dpsi_pixelization=af.Model(
        al.pc.DpsiPixelization,
        mesh=al.pc.RegularDpsiMesh(factor=2),
        regularization=af.Model(al.reg.MaternKernel, nu=2.5),
    ),
    src_pixelization=src_pixelization,
)

analysis = al.pc.DpsiSrcInvAnalysis(
    masked_imaging=masked_imaging,
    lens_start=lens_smooth,
    source_start=source_start,
    src_image_mesh=src_image_mesh,
)

search = af.Nautilus(path_prefix="potential_correction", name="dpsi_src", n_live=100)
result = search.fit(model=dpsi_model, analysis=analysis)
```

## Branch — interferometer data

The benchmark detections are radio/VLBI, and `al.pc` supports `Interferometer` datasets in
visibility space through the **sparse-operator (w-tilde) route**, whose cost scales with
real-space mask pixels independent of the visibility count — which is what makes ALMA/VLBI
volumes tractable. The entry points are `al.pc.FitDpsiSrcInterferometer` and
`al.pc.IterFitDpsiSrcInterferometer`.

Two practices carry over from the published pipelines: **warm-start the iterative engine
from the one-shot solution** (pass `x0=fit.src_dpsi_slim`, so it refines inside the right
basin rather than searching from zero), and let the evidence re-optimize the regularization
strengths each step (`reg_optimize_every=1`) rather than fixing them. Note also that the
real-space mask stays a filled circle — it defines the FFT extent — while the corrections
are restricted to an arc-tracing `dpsi_mask`.

The certified end-to-end recipe is
`autolens_workspace:scripts/interferometer/features/advanced/potential_correction/start_here.py`.
[`al_build_interferometer_model`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PyAutoLabs/autolens_assistant/main/skills/al_build_interferometer_model.md) covers getting the
dataset loaded in the first place.

  you want a *detection significance* for a specific perturber model; reach for corrections
  when you do not want to assume the perturber's form at all. In practice they pair: the
  $\delta\kappa$ peak tells you where to centre the grid search, and the grid search gives
  the peak an evidence ratio.
